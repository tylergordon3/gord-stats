Health-adjusted drafting - separating bad picks from hurt ones

Every hit rate on this page is really two things at once: whether the pick was any good, and whether they stayed on the field. This section separates them, and weighs each absence the way the homepage's injury tables do rather than counting every missed week the same. Weeks Lost counts the weeks in the first 13 where a drafted player recorded no stat line. High-Impact Weeks Lost are the ones that belonged to a pick from the first 2 rounds, or to a player scoring at weekly-starter pace (top 25% median weekly points among drafted players at his position that season). Est. Pts Lost prices every absence at the player's median week. Healthy Hit % is the hit rate counting only picks active at least 11 of 13 weeks — how good the picks were when they were actually available, which is the part a manager controls.

Jackson loses the most, sliding from 1st to 5th — only 48 weeks lost meant there was little bad luck inflating their raw number to begin with; Max gains the most, 8th to 6th. What it does not do is close the field up: the gap between best and worst widens from 32 points to 43. Bad luck explains a lot of the middle of this table and none of the bottom of it.

What this does and doesn't measure. Weeks lost counts absences of every kind — injury, benching, a release — not injuries specifically. The league's weekly injury reports (data/injuries) can't fill that gap: they list players ruled Out week by week, and a player lost for the season drops off the report entirely, so they under-count the absences that matter most.
