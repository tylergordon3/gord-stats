Draft DNA

Not how well each manager drafts — that's the Manager Draft Report — but how they draft. Positional timing, early-round shape, how much of the result came down to players simply staying on the field, and what the championship drafts had in common.

Sample size: 3 drafts, 381 picks in total, so roughly 38 picks per manager. That is enough to describe a habit and not enough to prove one — read the 'never before round N' lines as 'hasn't yet', not as a law. Kickers and defenses are excluded throughout.

Availability - separating bad picks from hurt ones

Every hit rate on this page is really two things at once: whether the pick was any good, and whether they stayed on the field. This section separates them. Weeks active counts the weeks in the first 13 where a drafted player recorded a stat line — a week they missed is a week their manager got nothing from that roster spot.

Availability decides almost everything

Weeks active Picks Became a starter
6 or fewer of 13 33 0%
7-10 of 13 82 16%
11-13 of 13 266 59%

Of the 33 picks who were active for 6 or fewer of 13 weeks, 0% finished as a weekly starter. Availability isn't a factor in draft success so much as a precondition for it.

Health-adjusted drafting

Healthy Hit % is the hit rate counting only picks who were active for at least 11 of 13 weeks — it asks how good the picks were when they were actually available, which is the part a manager controls.

Everett's picks sat out 88 player-weeks against 47 for Padgett — a gap of 3.2 full player-seasons across three drafts, none of it a drafting decision. Adjusting for it puts Colin on top at 73%.

Jackson loses the most, sliding from 1st to 5th — only 48 player-weeks lost meant there was little bad luck inflating their raw number to begin with; Max gains the most, 8th to 6th. What it does not do is close the field up: the gap between best and worst widens from 32 points to 43. Bad luck explains a lot of the middle of this table and none of the bottom of it.

What this does and doesn't measure. Weeks active counts absences of every kind — injury, benching, a release — not injuries specifically. The league's weekly injury reports (data/injuries) can't fill that gap: they list players ruled Out week by week, and a player lost for the season drops off the report entirely, so they under-count the absences that matter most. Nick Chubb's two-game 2023 and Brandon Aiyuk's lost 2025 appear nowhere in them. Weeks active catches both.

Champion Blueprint - how the title winners drafted

Champions taken from Sleeper's playoff bracket, then measured against everyone else who drafted that same year. Starter Hit % is the share of a manager's picks that finished inside their position's weekly starter pool (top 10 QB / 24 RB / 24 WR / 10 TE for a 10-team, 2-flex league). Weeks Lost / Pick is how many of the 13 weeks the average pick spent inactive. Avg Finish vs Pick = draft position minus fantasy finish (+ = picks outperformed their slots); Avg ADP Value = pick minus consensus ADP — draft-day context, not an outcome.

Across all 3 titles the champion beat the field's hit rate 3 of 3 times and got more finish out of their draft slots 2 of 3 times, but only led the field in draft-day value against ADP 2 of 3 times. On this sample, titles track picks that worked more than picks that looked like bargains on draft day — though three seasons cannot separate that from simple luck.

Health is not the explanation. The champion's picks were the healthier group in only 1 of 3 seasons, and 2025-2026's winner took the title with their picks missing 4.3 weeks apiece against the field's 2.1.
